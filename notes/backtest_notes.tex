\section*{Summary}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{New function} \texttt{buhl.pairs\_backtest}: trades one pair's
    standardized spread $z_t$ under the rule in 5(a). Your lag-day trend
    check is an optional entry filter.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{prob5\_driver.py}, lines 1--106, rewritten}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item reads the formation-period results (\texttt{sector\_pairs.dat},
        \texttt{kalman\_data.dat});
        \item downloads the test period once (2024-01-01 to 2026-10-01);
        \item builds the static and Kalman $z$-scores without lookahead;
        \item runs four variants per pair: static/Kalman spread, each with
        the trend filter off and on;
        \item records every trade in an inventory and a ledger.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trade tracking}: an \emph{inventory} (shares held at every
    close) and a \emph{ledger} of every purchase and sale. Sharpe ratio,
    drawdown, turnover, trade counts and transaction costs can all be computed
    from these two afterwards without rerunning the backtest (Section 3).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Data in.} The two formation-period files have one row per pair in
the same order. The driver checks this and stops with an error if the tickers
don't match (rerun \texttt{prob4\_driver.py} after any new sweep). The test
prices are downloaded in one call and stored as dictionaries of numpy arrays
on a shared date grid, the same pattern as Problem 4:
\begin{lstlisting}
tickers = sorted(set(ticker_a) | set(ticker_b))
ticker_prices = yf.download(tickers, start=start, end=end, auto_adjust=True,
                            progress=False)["Close"]
dates = ticker_prices.index
prices = {ticker: np.array(ticker_prices[ticker]) for ticker in tickers}
log_prices = {ticker: np.log(prices[ticker]) for ticker in tickers}
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{Spreads without lookahead.} For each pair, keep only the days
where both prices exist (\texttt{mask}). Then build:
\begin{gather*}
    z^{\text{static}}_t = \frac{\ln P^A_t - \alpha - \beta \ln P^B_t - \mu}{\sigma_{\text{stat}}},
    \qquad
    z^{\text{kalman}}_t = \frac{e_t - \mu_k}{\sigma_{\text{stat},k}},
\end{gather*}
where all of $(\alpha, \beta, \mu, \sigma_{\text{stat}})$ come from
\texttt{sector\_pairs.dat}. The Kalman spread $e_t$ continues the filter from
its state on the last formation day, with $(\mu_k, \sigma_{\text{stat},k})$
from \texttt{kalman\_data.dat}. $e_t$ is the one-step prediction error, so it
uses only $(\alpha_{t-1}, \beta_{t-1})$ and the prices at $t$.
\begin{lstlisting}
alpha_k, hr_k, spread_k, P_k, R_k, lPb_mean_k = db.kalman_spread(lPa, lPb,
    dt, kalman["sigma_alpha"][i], kalman["sigma_beta"][i],
    alpha0=kalman["alpha"][i], beta0=kalman["hedge_ratio"][i], P0=P0,
    R=kalman["R"][i], lPb_mean=kalman["lPb_mean"][i])
z_kalman = (spread_k - kalman["mu"][i])/kalman["stat_std_dev"][i]
\end{lstlisting}

\section{The trading rule: \texttt{buhl.pairs\_backtest}}

\begin{lstlisting}
def pairs_backtest(Pa, Pb, lPa, lPb, z, hedge_ratio, z_in=2., z_out=1.,
                   lag=10, dt=1./252, trend_filter=False):
\end{lstlisting}

The position state is $s \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ (short spread, flat, long spread).
The decision on day $t$ uses $z_t$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    State & Condition at close $t$ & Action at close $t+1$ \\
    \midrule
    flat & $z_t > z_{\text{in}}$ & $s=-1$: sell \$1 of A, buy \$$\beta_t$ of B \\
    flat & $z_t < -z_{\text{in}}$ & $s=+1$: buy \$1 of A, sell \$$\beta_t$ of B \\
    in a trade & $|z_t| < z_{\text{out}}$ & sell back the shares held, $s = 0$ \\
    in a trade & last valid day & forced close \\
    \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The defaults are $z_{\text{in}} = 2$ and $z_{\text{out}} = 1$, from your
comments.

\paragraph{Why these positions.} A long spread position pays
$\Delta\ln P^A - \beta\Delta\ln P^B = \Delta S$. When $z$ is very negative,
the spread is expected to rise back toward $\mu$, so we go long, and the
reverse when $z$ is very positive. \$1 of A against \$$\beta$ of B is the
dollar hedge that matches the hedge ratio, since $\beta$ is defined in log
prices.

\paragraph{No lookahead.} The signal is computed from the close on day $t$,
and the trade fills at the close on day $t+1$. No entry is allowed whose fill
would land on the last day. The first \texttt{lag} days are skipped so the
trend filter always has a full window.

\paragraph{Sizing.} The share counts are set at entry and held until exit:
\begin{gather*}
    n_A = \frac{s}{P^A_{t+1}}, \qquad n_B = -\frac{s\,\beta_t}{P^B_{t+1}}.
\end{gather*}
There is no daily re-hedging. For the Kalman variants, $\beta_t$ moves every
day, but it only enters through the $z$-score and the hedge at entry. That
keeps turnover (and costs) down. Daily re-hedging would be a different
strategy.

\paragraph{Trend filter.} With \texttt{trend\_filter=True}, an entry is only
taken if the leg being bought has fallen over the last \texttt{lag} days, using
your backward difference:
\begin{lstlisting}
lP_buy = lPa if (s == 1) else lPb
trend = np.sum(BFD(lP_buy[t-lag:t+1], lag+1, dt))
if (trend >= 0):
    s = 0
\end{lstlisting}
The sum of the backward differences telescopes to
$(\ln P_t - \ln P_{t-\text{lag}})/\Delta t$, the log return over the window.
Exits are never filtered, so a position is never kept open because of the
trend.

\section{How trades are tracked}

Two records are kept, following your inventory plus purchases/sales idea.

\paragraph{1. Inventory (the \texttt{inv}).} For each variant \texttt{v},
\texttt{shares\_a[v]} and \texttt{shares\_b[v]} are arrays of shape
(number of pairs $\times$ test days). Entry $[i, t]$ is the number of shares of
that leg held by pair $i$ at the close of day $t$. Positive is long and
negative is short.
\begin{lstlisting}
variants = [('static', False), ('static', True), ('kalman', False),
            ('kalman', True)]
shares_a = {v: np.zeros((num_pairs, nt_all)) for v in variants}
shares_b = {v: np.zeros((num_pairs, nt_all)) for v in variants}
\end{lstlisting}
Because holdings are stored for every day, the daily P\&L is a single product:
\begin{gather*}
    \text{PnL}_t = \sum_i n^A_{i,t-1}\,(P^A_{i,t} - P^A_{i,t-1})
                 + n^B_{i,t-1}\,(P^B_{i,t} - P^B_{i,t-1}).
\end{gather*}
\textbf{Missing days.} The backtest runs only on a pair's valid days, so
results are mapped back to the full date grid. A missing price in the middle
of the window must not close the position, so each missing day takes the
holdings of the last valid day:
\begin{lstlisting}
full_range = np.arange(days[0], days[-1]+1)
last_valid = np.searchsorted(days, full_range, side='right') - 1
shares_a[v][i, full_range] = sa[last_valid]
\end{lstlisting}
When computing P\&L, also forward-fill prices over missing days. This fixed a
real mismatch: LBRDA has a gap partway through the test period.

\paragraph{2. Ledger (purchases and sales).} Every transaction leg is one row
of a numpy structured array:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    field & meaning \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{pair} & row index into the pairs files \\
    \texttt{day} & index into \texttt{dates} (the fill day) \\
    \texttt{leg}, \texttt{ticker} & \texttt{'A'} or \texttt{'B'} and its ticker \\
    \texttt{shares} & signed: $> 0$ purchase, $< 0$ sale \\
    \texttt{price} & fill price \\
    \texttt{branch}, \texttt{trend\_filter} & which variant made the trade \\
    \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{lstlisting}
purchases = ledger[ledger['shares'] > 0]
sales = ledger[ledger['shares'] < 0]
\end{lstlisting}
A round trip is four rows: the entry and the exit of both legs.

\paragraph{What each metric needs.}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.62\textwidth}}
    \toprule
    Quantity & From \\
    \midrule
    daily P\&L, Sharpe, drawdown & inventory and forward-filled prices (formula above) \\
    number of trades & ledger rows / 4, per variant \\
    turnover & $\sum |\texttt{shares}\cdot\texttt{price}|$ over the ledger \\
    transaction costs & $c\,|\texttt{shares}\cdot\texttt{price}|$ subtracted on each row's \texttt{day} \\
    cost sweep (5b) & repeat the line above for several $c$; no new backtest \\
    \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For example, net daily P\&L at cost rate $c$ for variant \texttt{v}:
\begin{lstlisting}
in_v = (ledger['branch'] == v[0]) & (ledger['trend_filter'] == v[1])
cost = np.zeros(nt_all)
np.add.at(cost, ledger['day'][in_v],
          c*np.abs(ledger['shares'][in_v]*ledger['price'][in_v]))
pnl_net = pnl_gross - cost
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Verification}

\paragraph{Synthetic path} (40 days, $\beta = 1.3$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item a $z = 2.5$ signal on day 12 fills on day 13: short 0.0099 shares of
    A at \$101 (\$1) and long 0.0265 shares of B at \$49 (\$1.30). The exit
    signal on day 18 fills on day 19;
    \item a long entry that never meets the exit condition is closed on the
    last day;
    \item the trend filter blocks a long-spread entry while A is rising, and
    allows a short-spread entry while B is falling;
    \item a signal on the second-to-last day doesn't open a trade;
    \item each leg's shares sum to zero.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Full run} (931 pairs, 689 test days):
\begin{itemize}
    \item every (pair, leg, variant) nets to zero shares, and nothing is held
    on the last day;
    \item the total Kalman P\&L summed from the inventory equals the ledger's
    cash flows $-\sum \texttt{shares}\cdot\texttt{price}$ exactly
    ($-145.263883$ both ways, before costs, at \$1 notional per trade).
\end{itemize}
